THE BASICS

What is a car wrap?

A wrap is a thin, flexible vinyl film applied over your car’s paint to change its color or finish — gloss, satin, matte, metallic and more. Your original paint stays underneath, so the change isn’t permanent.

Wrap, PPF, tint, ceramic coating — what’s the difference?
  • Vinyl wrap changes the color or finish of your car.
  • Paint protection film (PPF) is a thicker, usually clear film that protects your paint from rock chips and scratches.
  • Window tint is a film on the glass for privacy, less heat and less glare.
  • Ceramic coating is a liquid layer that adds gloss and makes the car easier to wash. It does not stop rock chips.

Many customers combine them — for example, a wrap with ceramic coating, or front PPF with window tint.

VINYL WRAPS

Will a wrap damage my paint?

On factory paint in good condition, a professionally installed wrap doesn’t damage the paint when it’s removed properly — it actually shields it from sun and light scratches. Repainted panels, rock chips or peeling clear coat can lift during removal, so we check your paint before quoting.

How long does a wrap last?

Quality wrap films from brands like Avery Dennison and 3M typically last around 5 to 7 years. Strong sun, outdoor parking and harsh car washes shorten that; garage parking and gentle hand washing help it last. Our wraps come with a 5-year installation warranty.

Can I go back to my original color later?

Yes. A wrap can be removed and your car returns to its original paint. Removal is quoted separately.

Why wrap instead of painting?

A quality full respray in a new color usually costs more, takes longer and is permanent. A wrap is reversible, protects the factory paint underneath and gives you finishes that are hard to get with paint, like satin or color-shift.

Can you wrap just part of the car?

Yes — roofs, mirrors, hoods, stripes, chrome delete (turning shiny trim black) and more. These are priced individually; send us a photo and your idea.

PAINT PROTECTION FILM (PPF)

What does PPF protect against?

Rock chips, road debris, light scratches, and stains from bugs and bird droppings. Many films “self-heal” fine swirl marks with heat. It can’t stop deep impacts or accident damage. Most people choose full front (bumper, hood, fenders, mirrors — where chips happen most) or full body.

Will people be able to see the film?

Clear gloss PPF is nearly invisible and keeps your factory look. Matte PPF turns glossy paint into a satin finish, and color PPF changes the color while protecting the paint.

WINDOW TINT

What do the tint percentages mean?

Tint is measured in VLT — the percentage of light that passes through. A lower number is darker: 5% is very dark (“limo”), 20% is dark, 35% is medium, 50% is light and 70% is barely visible.

How dark can I go in California?

In California, the front side windows must stay light — they need to let in at least 70% of light. Rear side windows and the back glass can be any darkness if your car has both side mirrors. A non-reflective strip is allowed on the top 4 inches of the windshield. Medical exemptions exist. We’ll go over the rules and recommend a shade for your car.

When can I roll my windows down?

Keep them up for 3 to 5 days while the film cures. A little haze or small water spots during that time is normal. See the aftercare guide.
